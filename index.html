UNDERSTANDING SOLAR AND GEOMAGNETIC CONDITIONS

SOLAR WIND

A stream of charged particles released by the Sun. Its speed and density can influence Earth's space environment.

GEOMAGNETIC ACTIVITY

The K-index describes disturbances in Earth's magnetic field. Higher values indicate stronger geomagnetic activity.

AURORAS

Interactions between charged particles and Earth's upper atmosphere can create colourful auroras near the polar regions.

SPACE KNOWLEDGE BASE
DISCOVER THE SCIENCE BEHIND EARTH ORBIT

THE ISS

The International Space Station travels around Earth roughly every 90 minutes, completing about 16 orbits each day.

STARLINK

Starlink satellites operate in low Earth orbit and form a network designed to provide broadband internet connectivity.

GPS

GPS satellites orbit Earth at an altitude of approximately 20,200 kilometres.

WEATHER WATCH

Weather satellites help scientists observe clouds, storms, atmospheric conditions and changing weather patterns.

GEOSTATIONARY ORBIT

A geostationary satellite circles Earth above the equator once per sidereal day, appearing nearly fixed over one longitude.

EARTH OBSERVATION

Earth-observation satellites collect information about oceans, forests, ice, agriculture and land surfaces.

LOW EARTH ORBIT

Low Earth orbit is commonly used by imaging, research and communications satellites.

ORBITAL DEBRIS

Old spacecraft and fragments can remain in orbit. Tracking objects helps support space situational awareness.

THE MOON

The Moon is Earth's natural satellite. It takes about 27.3 days to orbit Earth relative to the stars.
